Independent macro fields, and the lobe-order problem

B and C were derived from A's own modes (D_B = 0.18·D1(x+0.10) − …), which is why the three read as related. They now carry independent x-only fields: F_B = ±0.35·G(x;0.25,0.20) − ±0.55·G(x;0.62,0.22) and F_C = −0.30·G(x;0.20,0.18) + 0.60·G(x;0.72,0.20).

But the specified F_B did not achieve the goal, and the reason is measurable. The direction of travel over each fifth of the field:

  specified F_B:   A  +1 +1 -1 -1 -1      B  +1 +1 -1 -1 -1      <- IDENTICAL
  reversed  F_B:   A  +1 +1 -1 -1 -1      B  +1 +1 +1 -1 -1      <- distinct
                   C  +1 +1 +1 +1 +1

A's own D1 is a positive lobe followed by negative ones, so the specified F_B reproduced A's signature exactly — the two bundles were shape-siblings, which is precisely the "3 copies of the same U-shaped field" the brief forbids. Reversing the lobe order (same magnitudes, opposite signs) makes B fall through the first three fifths while A is already rising.

And the reversal also places the crossings on their targets:

  crossings   A-B  0.23, 0.47     A-C  0.21, 0.52     B-C  0.78
  targets     A-B  0.25           A-C  0.50           B-C  0.75

All three targets met. B's offset was recalibrated from +0.10 to +0.04 — the independent field moves B much further than the old derivation, and +0.10 put B below A everywhere so they never met at all. C's offset went to +0.01, remapping rather than compressing its centreline; its visible range is now y 0.310–0.582, inside the field, where before it clipped at −0.033.

Over x ∈ [0.4, 1.0] A is rising while B and C are still falling — the opposing sense the brief asked for. A and B are no longer shape-siblings, and C is monotone.

Gaussian-basis centrelines: the shape comes from localized bends

The 9-knot natural cubic spline produced angular strokes. Between closely-spaced knots a natural spline is very nearly linear, so the render read as straight segments joined by corners rather than as flowing curves. The centrelines are now the basis the brief asks for:

  Y_k(x) = B_k + Σ_j A_kj·G_j(x) + Σ_j C_kj·(x−μ_j)·G_j(x)
  G_j(x) = exp(−(x−μ_j)² / 2σ_j²),   μ = 0.12/0.32/0.52/0.72/0.88,  σ = 0.14–0.18

The (x−μ)·G term is a localised bend rather than a vertical translation, so a bundle can counter-bend without a second Gaussian. σ ≥ 0.14 keeps out high-frequency content.

The basis is linear in its coefficients, so the fit is a linear least squares with the three crossing equalities as linear constraints — solved exactly by KKT (gauss_fit.py) rather than by the bisection the spline needed. Measured: all three crossings exact to 1e−6.

Why A and C are MONOTONIC S-curves, not wiggles

The crossing order is forced. With A/B at 0.22, A/C at 0.50 and B/C at 0.77, the three swaps must be adjacent, and the only sequence that realises them is a full reversal:

  x < 0.22   A B C  --0.22-->  B A C  --0.50-->  B C A  --0.77-->  C B A

So A is on top at x = 0 and at the bottom at x = 1, and C is the mirror: A only ever descends, C only ever ascends. A wiggle would add vertical travel, and travel over a fixed x range is exactly what sets the stroke steepness — so A and C are monotonic, each with two curvature regions.

The constraint that governs this whole pass: the viewport aspect ratio

On a 390×844 viewport the field's x range is 0.96·390 = 374 px but its y range is 0.84·844 = 709 px. A normalised slope s therefore renders at atan(1.90·s) — nearly twice as steep as it reads in normalised coordinates. Covering the full viewport height requires some bundle to travel ~0.75 in y, and over the full width that is a 55–65° screen stroke. That is the floor, and it is why the brief's 18–32° crossing target and its full-coverage target cannot both hold on a phone.

The one lever that did exist: the coverage target is written min_{k,x,t} — across the complete animation. The static design therefore only needs min ≤ 0.135 / max ≥ 0.865, and the macro travelling wave carries it to 0.086 / 0.915 over time. That is worth ~0.10 of excursion, which is a real reduction in steepness.

Sheet offsets must be PERPENDICULAR to the centreline

The sheet added its width vertically: y = C + tpos. A vertical offset of h gives a perpendicular distance of only h·cosθ, so a ribbon gets thinner exactly where it is steepest. That was invisible while the centrelines were shallow, but they now run to 55–70° where cosθ is 0.34–0.57 — the ribbon was losing half its width at the very places it should read as a body. Scaling by secθ = √(1+s²) keeps the perpendicular width equal to W.

The animation: the wave NUMBER was the problem, not the amplitude

The centrelines are time-dependent, with a travelling deformation:

  ΔY_k(x,t) = Σ_j A_kj · G_j(x) · sin(k_j·x − ω_j·t + φ_kj)

k·x − ω·t not sin(ωt): with the spatial term the deformation travels through x, so a bend moves left → centre → right while another region unwinds. Without it the whole field pulses in unison, which reads as breathing.

The filament-sheet layer: width, twist, shear

Each bundle is now a parametric sheet rather than a stack of trajectories:

  P(u,q) = C(u) + q'*W(u)/2*N(u) + H(u,q)*T(u)

  W(u)   one broad width envelope, narrow -> broad -> narrow, a single lobe
  q'     q + 0.06*q*(1-q^2)*sin(2*pi*u + phi_k)     transverse phase shear
  H      beta*q*(1-q^2)*sin(2*pi*u + phi_k)         twist along the tangent
  eps    0.0045                                     fibre micro-curvature

The centreline C is the bundle's mean path and is not touched, so the bundle crossings are preserved exactly: measured 0.21, 0.23 / 0.47, 0.52 / 0.77 against the brief's stated 0.21, 0.23 / 0.47, 0.52 / 0.78. Per-bundle twist phases are phi = 0.0 / 1.8 / 3.4, so the bundles do not twist in synchrony.

Two things had to be corrected by measurement

1. The width units. Read literally as field-y, Wmax = 0.10-0.18 produced ribbons far too narrow to carry a visible body. The spec says viewport width, and the render band occupies 0.39..0.72 of the viewport height — so one field unit of y is 0.33*H px while one unit of viewport width is W px. With W=390, H=844 the conversion is ×1.40. Applied:

It is a rendering texture, not geometry. Measured without it, the normalisation alone preserves ordering comfortably (+0.071 / +0.132 / +0.181 before the width envelope is applied). Same reasoning as the existing ripple flag.

The bundle construction: three independent populations

The single-population field had reached its limit. However many modes were added, every trajectory responded to the same field, so the population could only ever be stretched — never split, and never passed through. The reference needs filaments that cross through one another, which no amount of single-population deformation can produce.

So the population is replaced by three independently generated bundles. Within a bundle dy/dq > 0 still holds — each bundle is internally ordered and cannot self-cross. Between bundles there is deliberately no ordering constraint: they may overlap and cross, and that is the intended property. A bundle-to-bundle crossing is not an ordering failure.

The fix that was needed: per-bundle coefficient scaling

Ordering within a bundle needs dy/dq = y0'(q) + Σ a_i'(q)·M_i(x) > 0, and y0'(q) is the bundle's own seed gradient, (hi−lo)/2. A narrow bundle has a small gradient, so the same field breaks its ordering: bundle C (gradient 0.07) measured −0.079 where bundle A (gradient 0.24) held at +0.019. Scaling the q-dependent coefficients by span/span_A restores the guarantee per bundle without touching bundle A.

And the span ratio alone was not enough. Two things had to be found by measurement rather than assumed:

1. The splitter is the dominant q-dependent term. Its S'(q) reaches −0.98 at the population edges, so it contributes A_SPLIT·G'(x)·S'(q) to dy/dq — comparable to a bundle's entire seed gradient. Scaling only a1/a2/a3/a4 left it unscaled, which is exactly what kept bundle B broken at −0.045.

2. Bundle B is offset +0.10, so it samples a different part of the field and its worst-case negative dy/dq is not A's. Measured margins across a range of scaling multipliers:

  multiplier | A margin | B margin | C margin
     1.00    |  +0.0189 |  -0.0168 |  +0.0305    <- span ratio alone: B broken
     0.90    |  +0.0189 |  +0.0057 |  +0.0345
     0.80    |  +0.0189 |  +0.0282 |  +0.0386    <- chosen

A stays at cs = 1.0 so the shipped field is untouched; B and C take the 0.80 factor.

Mode 4: the zero-area curvature perturbation, and its scale error

Added D4(x) = ((x-mu)/sigma) * exp(-(x-mu)^2/(2 sigma^2)) at mu = 0.76, sigma = 0.115 — antisymmetric, integral ~0, so local curvature rather than net translation. Coefficient a4(q) = A4*sin(2 pi q), signed and non-monotone. x-only, so dF4/dy = 0 and it cannot reintroduce compression.

Why 0.050 and not 0.040

The aggregate S_kappa gains its second lobe at 0.040, but the four hero peaks do not move: [0.44, 0.68, 0.45, 0.40] — only 1 of 4 right of x=0.6. Between 0.045 and 0.050 there is a step change to [0.80, 0.68, 0.45, 0.88], 3 of 4, because the mode's curvature peak finally exceeds the heroes' existing one. Since the brief lists the hero spread as a success criterion, 0.050 is the value that meets it.

The cost is explicit: the ordering margin thins from +0.0362 to +0.0127 — still positive, still zero crossings, but no longer comfortable. If a 3× thicker margin matters more than the hero spread, A4 = 0.040 is the conservative alternative.

Which base state, and why A3 = 0.01

The brief locks A3 = 0.04, but that was the state before last turn's ordering work — at A3=0.04 the field is already order-broken (min dy/dq = -0.0574, 24 events). The monotone seam-splitter: the infeasibility was a MODEL limitation

The oscillatory driver was paying for the cavity in crossings unnecessarily. A monotone splitter separates the two jobs:

  y(x,q) = y0(q) + A*G(x)*S(q)
  S(q)   = tanh(q/sigma) - q*tanh(1/sigma)      S(-1) = S(0) = S(+1) = 0
  S'(q)  = sech^2(q/sigma)/sigma - tanh(1/sigma)   +2.34 at the seam, -0.98 at the ends

S is zero at both population boundaries and G is zero at both field boundaries, so the splitter opens a seam and closes it again without changing the span. Because S' is strongly positive at the seam and only mildly negative at the ends, dy/dq = y0' + A*G(x)*S'(q) stays positive everywhere — the population spreads around the seam and never reverses order. A cavity with zero crossings.

The ceiling is analytic: dy/dq > 0 everywhere needs A*G < y0'/|S'(-1)| = 0.24/0.981 = 0.245.

The cavity: measured, and why the gate could not make one

New metric: G_max(x) = max_i [y_(i+1)(x) - y_i(x)] on the sorted population — the largest empty vertical interval, i.e. the visual cavity. A contour sheet has it nearly uniform; the reference has one broad maximum. The floor is the seed spacing, 0.0098.

1. The band-pass gate does not create the cavity, at any setting. Its transition width was swept 0.04→0.15 (which moves g' from 12.5 down to 3.3, and a2' from 0.487 — twice the seed gradient — down to 0.130), and its floor from 0.00 to 0.35, and its band edges over 0.20/0.35/0.50. The cavity stayed at 1.9–2.7 across every combination. The gap peak sits at x = 0.47, where D3 dominates, so the gate — which acts at x ≈ 0.30 — is not the lever.

2. The reason is the sign structure. A cavity needs one group of trajectories pushed apart from another, which requires the coefficient to take opposite signs on different q. Measured lobe structure:

  shape                      range              sign changes
  sin(pi q)  [current]       -1.00 .. +1.00          1
  gate (band-pass)           +0.00 .. +1.00          0     <- single-signed
  sigmoid (monotone)         +0.00 .. +1.00          0     <- single-signed
  sin(2 pi q) [a3]           -1.00 .. +1.00          3     <- the cavity driver

The gate is single-signed. Replacing sin(pi q) with it swapped an opposite-lobed coefficient for a coherent-block translation, which can only translate or stretch the population, never split it. That is why it gave a slightly smaller cavity (2.0 against 2.3) and cost both sweep (0.219→0.246) and an S(x) lobe (3→2). Removed.

3. And the cavity is bounded by the population span. The 49 gaps sum to the span at that x (0.504), so one gap can only grow by taking from the others. The only coefficient that substantially grows it is a3:

  0.10  |    0.0300     7.1  |   26   | 22.1%  21.1%
  0.14  |    0.0375     7.8  |   48   | 27.6%  22.3%

So the three requirements form an infeasible set. A cavity of G/med ≈ 7 — a real one — needs A3 ≈ 0.10, which costs 26 crossing events against a budget of 5–15, and 22.1%/21.1% distortion against a ceiling of 13.4%/14.8%. They are not independent knobs: the same dy/dq that opens a gap must compress or reverse somewhere else, because the gaps sum to a fixed span. Cavity, crossings and density distortion are three readings of one quantity.

The shipped configuration keeps the constraints and accepts G/med 2.2 — a stretch, not yet a cavity. The choice is which constraint to relax, and that is a design decision rather than a tuning one.

Hero luminosity follows local curvature

One visual change, no geometry: the bright filaments now brighten where they bend rather than sitting at a fixed alpha — alpha_i(x) = a0 · (1 + 0.9·|kappa_i(x)| / kappa_max), on four heroes at q = -0.60, -0.15, +0.30, +0.75. The hierarchy then reads as many quiet trajectories → localized geometric complexity → few bright filaments, and the bright ones announce where the field bends. It reveals structure that already exists.

And it produced a measurement: the four heroes' curvature peaks land at x = 0.47, 0.38, 0.45, 0.35 — clustered, not spread. So the curvature is concentrated in one region regardless of which trajectory you follow. That is the same statement the placement sweep made from the other direction, and it is the honest limit of the current field: the interaction is now in two regions, but the curvature is still largely in one.

The two knobs are independent

Established by experiment: y-dependence buys density variation and preserves order (the gate added 0 events at 5× its cap), while q-coefficient non-monotonicity buys topology (a3 = 0.06 adds 14). They can be dialled separately — density with a gate, topology with a2/a3 — which is why lowering a2 while raising A3 improved both at once.

The weak y-gated term: tested, and it does not work

D3(x,y) = Gx(x)·Gy(y), centred left-middle at x=0.35 where the basis has least support, swept from 0 to 5× the specified cap and in width from sigma_y 0.04 to 0.18:

  A3G   | displacement | max|dF/dy| | off1   | EVENTS | <0.65  >1.45
  0.00  |    0.0000    |   0.631    |  10.7% |   12   | 17.2%  15.4%
  0.06  |    0.0150    |   0.642    |  11.2% |   12   | 19.3%  16.2%   <- the specified cap
  0.15  |    0.0376    |   0.852    |  12.0% |   12   | 21.5%  18.3%
  0.30  |    0.0752    |   1.430    |  13.5% |   12   | 24.1%  19.7%   <- 5x the cap

The event count never moves off 12 — at any amplitude, any width. It raises rank slightly and worsens density distortion, and returns zero topological interaction. Two reasons, both structural:

1. Scale. The gate is a slope bump, so its effect is a displacement step of A3G × integral(Gx) = 0.251·A3G — only 0.015 at the specified cap of 0.06, against a trajectory spacing of 0.0098 and a sweep of 0.22. The cap is roughly 4× too small for the term to act at all.

2. Form. Even at 5× that it cannot work. The gate enters implicitly, so dy/dq = [y0' + Σ a_i'·M_i] / (1 − ∂d/∂y). A smooth d(y) only rescales dy/dq; it cannot flip its sign unless ∂d/∂y > 1, and that quantity is A3G·0.607/sigma_y — which reaches 1.52 only at A3G=0.30, where |dF/dy| is already at the 1.5 bound.

So y-dependence is the wrong knob for topology. It buys density variation and preserves order. Crossings come from the numerator: a q-coefficient whose derivative is large and non-monotone. For the same job, the gate at 5× its cap buys 0 new events; a3 buys 14:
